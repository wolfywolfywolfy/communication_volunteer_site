Lessons from COMM 2110!

Small Group Communication Concepts

This page and its companion flyer were created as part of a Weber State University communication course. Here are three useful ideas we learned!

01

Make great teams!

Group members take on different roles. These are patterns of behavior, not permanent labels for people.

Task roles

Help finish the job. An information seeker asks questions, a recorder keeps notes, and an expediter helps the group stay on track.

Maintenance roles

Help people work well together. A supporter encourages others; a harmonizer helps ease tension.

Unproductive roles

A monopolizer takes too much speaking time; a blocker stops progress. Focus on changing behaviors, not blaming people.

Further reading: Group Member Roles, §5.2 ↗

02

Try a perception check!

Someone acts differently than you expected. Rather than guess what it means, ask them with curiosity.

  1. 1. Describe what happenedState what you observed, without judging.
  2. 2. Offer two possibilitiesSuggest two reasonable interpretations without declaring either correct.
  3. 3. Ask and listenRequest clarification, then update your understanding.

Example at a volunteer meeting: “I noticed you were quiet while we picked shifts. Are you still deciding which time works, or did we move through the schedule too quickly? What's going on?”

Why it helps: It gives someone space to explain their own perspective, preventing misunderstandings and reducing defensiveness.

Further reading: Improving Perception, §2.4 ↗

03

Build a supportive atmosphere!

The way we speak can help teammates feel welcome—or make them feel defensive. Compare these communication styles.

Less supportiveMore supportive
Evaluation“You're messy!”
Description“The supplies aren't sorted yet.”
Control“Do it my way.”
Problem orientation“How can we divide this work?”
StrategyHide your reason for asking.
SpontaneityExplain your intentions openly.
Neutrality“That's not my problem.”
Empathy“That sounds frustrating.”
Superiority“I know better than you.”
Equality“Let's hear everyone's ideas.”
Certainty“This is the only right way.”
Provisionalism“This might work. What do you think?”
